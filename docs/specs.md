## 1. Project name and overview

### Project name

KisanRakshak

### Overview

KisanRakshak is a weather-aware crop protection assistant designed for Indian farmers. It uses actual weather forecasts and crop-specific information to identify potential weather risks and provide preventive guidance in simple Hindi or English. Farmers can enter their location, select their crop and provide their sowing date and manually select their current growth stage to receive personalised advisories. The initial version will focus on farmers in Saharanpur, Uttar Pradesh, growing sugarcane and wheat, with scope for future expansion to more crops and regions.

## 2. Problem statement

### Problem

Farmers often receive general weather forecasts but may not know how upcoming weather conditions could affect their crops. Different crops and growth stages respond differently to rainfall, temperature and other weather changes. This makes it difficult for farmers to identify potential risks and take preventive action at the right time. KisanRakshak aims to bridge this gap by converting weather forecasts into crop-specific risk alerts and practical guidance.

## 6. Processing rules and business logic

**2. Weather Data Processing**

- Retrieve temperature, rainfall, humidity and wind speed.

**3. Crop-Specific Risk Detection**

- Classify detected risks as Low, Moderate or High.

**Crop-Specific Risk Research and MVP Decisions**
**1. Farmer Crop Inputs**

- For both sugarcane and wheat, collect the sowing date and allow the farmer to manually select the current growth stage.
- Use the manually selected growth stage as the primary input for risk detection.
- Use the sowing date as supporting context. Do not automatically override the farmer's selected stage.

**2. Sugarcane Growth Stages**

Support the following stages:
- Germination
- Tillering
- Grand Growth
- Maturity / Ripening

Potential weather risks include heat stress, cold stress, waterlogging and water stress.

Research on sugarcane in Uttar Pradesh identifies temperature and moisture as important factors across its growth stages. ICAR-IIFSR guidance highlights the importance of drainage during early growth and adequate water availability during grand growth.

Temperature observations from research must not automatically be treated as universal alert thresholds. Numerical thresholds, forecast duration and severity must be validated before automated classification.

Excessive rainfall may generate a qualitative waterlogging warning, but rainfall alone must not be treated as confirmation of actual field waterlogging.

**3. Wheat Growth Stages**

Support the following simplified stages:
- Early Growth (emergence and seedling)
- Crown Root Development (CRI)
- Vegetative Growth (tillering)
- Reproductive Stage (booting and flowering)
- Grain Development / Maturity (milking, dough formation and physiological maturity)

Potential weather risks include terminal heat stress, flowering heat stress, low-temperature stress and excess rainfall.

The IMD KALP framework provides an example of maximum temperature above 30°C during grain filling as a potential heat-risk condition. This can be used as a source-attributed candidate rule, subject to validation for the Saharanpur region.

Other temperature thresholds found in research must be stored with their original source, applicable growth stage and measurement context. Do not combine different research thresholds into one rule without validation.

**4. Risk Rule Implementation**

- Maintain separate rule sets for sugarcane and wheat.
- Store each rule's crop, growth stage, weather parameter, threshold, unit, duration, severity, source and precautionary action.
- Distinguish validated operational thresholds from research observations and thresholds requiring further validation.
- Only validated rules may automatically assign Low, Moderate or High severity.
- Where only qualitative evidence is available, display a clearly labelled potential-risk advisory without inventing a numerical threshold.
- If the required data or threshold is unavailable, display an insufficient-data or validation-pending status.
- Do not predict confirmed crop damage, actual field waterlogging or disease presence using weather forecasts alone.

**5. Initial MVP Exclusions**

- Do not implement automated sugarcane lodging prediction until a suitable wind threshold is verified.

## 8. Error handling and edge cases

### Error handling and edge cases

The application should handle the following situations gracefully:

1. **Invalid or Missing Inputs**
- If the farmer does not enter required details, display a clear message asking them to complete the fields.
- If the location or sowing date is invalid, ask the user to correct it.

2. **Location Errors**
- If the entered location cannot be identified, allow the farmer to enter another location.
- If GPS permission is denied, allow manual location entry.

3. **Weather API Failure**
- If Open-Meteo is unavailable or returns an error, display a message that weather data could not be fetched.
- Provide an option to retry.
- Never show fabricated weather information.

4. **Missing or Outdated Weather Data**
- If required weather parameters are missing, show an insufficient-data message.
- Clearly indicate when forecast data is outdated.
- Do not generate confident risk alerts using incomplete or stale information.

5. **Unsupported Crop**
- If a farmer selects a crop that is not supported in the initial version, explain that it is not currently available and show the supported crop options.

6. **AI API Failure**
- If Gemini is unavailable, times out or returns an invalid response, show the detected risk information from the backend wherever available.
- Display a fallback message explaining that the AI explanation is temporarily unavailable.
- Provide an option to retry.

7. **No Significant Risk Detected**
- If no predefined risk conditions are triggered, display that no significant weather risk was detected in the available forecast.
- Do not claim that the crop is completely safe.

8. **Database or Network Failure**
- If advisory details cannot be saved or retrieved, notify the user.
- Provide a retry option wherever possible.

9. **Language or Translation Issues**
- If a response cannot be generated in the selected language, display a clear message or provide a safe fallback in the available language.

The application should never crash or display misleading information because of an API failure, missing data or unexpected user input.
